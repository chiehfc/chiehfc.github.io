I
Section 01 · Proposal

The Proposal · What Kind of Meetup This Is

Theme & Vibe

One Day in the Jianghu — from teahouse to stage

We are taking the most everyday side of Where Winds Meet — resting at a teahouse, catching up with your sect, listening to music, sparring, sharing a hot meal — and staging it in Los Angeles. Lunch at a Thai restaurant stands in for the teahouse stop, the afternoon KTV room becomes the stage, and a banquet-style dinner closes the day. No panels and no scripted programme: people from the same guild meeting, eating and playing for one full day.

Main Activities

Sing, quiz, play, feast — all running at once

  • Jianghu trivia: a 30-question Where Winds Meet quiz, teams buzzing in, keepsakes for the top three
  • Anniversary watch-along: official anniversary videos and player retrospectives on the room screen
  • Karaoke: game OST and wuxia classics get first pick of the queue
  • Mahjong table: running all afternoon, scored with chips only — no real money
  • Co-op session: parties formed on the spot for dungeons and world bosses on mobile
  • Badges & group photo: name badges with in-game IDs at the door, group photo at dinner
Venue

Three stops across greater Los Angeles, minutes apart

  • Lunch  Very Thai (Los Angeles)
  • Afternoon  D'vine plus KTV (San Gabriel) · 5-hour room, all-inclusive
  • Dinner  Momo Bistro (San Gabriel)

The afternoon and evening stops are both in San Gabriel — the heart of the Chinese community in Southern California, where parking is easy, the food suits the group, and each leg is a ten-minute drive.

Why this is great

The players are already here — nobody has run the first one

  • Southern California has a large Chinese-speaking player base, yet almost no Where Winds Meet meetups — this would be the first
  • One day end to end: no time off work, and out-of-town Wanderers can fly in and out the same day
  • About $150 a head covers three meals and a five-hour room; every expense is published and all surplus refunded
  • The itinerary is the game’s daily life — eating, music, sparring, tiles — lifted into the real world
  • Capped at 15 so everyone actually gets to talk, rather than a crowd where you meet no one
